Every H-2A deadline is counted back from one date: your **date of need**, the first day you need workers on the farm. Miss a step and your crew can arrive late, or not at all. Here is how a season comes together.

## The H-2A calendar at a glance

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| About 4 months before your start date | Plan your dates, crew size, jobs, pay and housing |
| 60 to 75 days before | Your job order goes to your state workforce agency |
| At least 45 days before | Your application goes to the Department of Labor (DOL) |
| About 30 days before | DOL makes its decision |
| After DOL approves | Petition filed with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) |
| After USCIS approves | Workers apply for visas at the consulate |
| Right before your start date | Workers travel to your farm |

## Step by step

**1. Plan (about 4 months out).** Decide your start and end dates, how many workers you need, what they will do, and where they will live. If you used H-2A last season, start from that file.

**2. Job order to your state (60 to 75 days out).** The job order (Form ETA-790 and its addendum) lays out the work, pay, hours, housing and other terms. It goes to your state workforce agency no more than 75 and no fewer than 60 days before your date of need ([20 CFR 655.121](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.121)). The state posts the job so U.S. workers can apply first.

**3. Application to DOL (at least 45 days out).** The Application for Temporary Employment Certification (Form ETA-9142A) is filed in DOL's online system, FLAG, at least 45 days before your date of need ([20 CFR 655.130](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.130)). If DOL finds a problem, it sends a notice of deficiency, and you have **5 business days** to fix it ([20 CFR 655.141](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.141)).

**4. DOL decision (about 30 days out).** When everything is in order, DOL aims to decide no later than 30 days before your date of need.

**5. USCIS petition.** With DOL's certification, a petition (Form I-129) goes to USCIS asking to bring in your workers.

**6. Consulate and travel.** Once USCIS approves, workers apply for visas, usually at a U.S. consulate in Mexico, and then travel to your farm.

## What usually causes delays

- **Housing inspections.** Your housing has to be inspected and approved before the season. Book the inspection early.
- **Missing details in the job order.** Incomplete or inconsistent terms lead to deficiency notices.
- **Slow responses.** A 5-business-day window goes fast in a busy week.
- **Consulate backlogs.** Appointment availability changes, especially in spring.
