Pay statements and payroll records are the first thing an investigator looks at. Missing items on a pay stub are one of the most common H-2A findings.

## What every pay statement must show

On or before each payday, every worker gets a statement with ([20 CFR 655.122(k)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)):

- [ ] total earnings for the pay period
- [ ] hourly rate and piece rate, if any
- [ ] hours offered, with guaranteed hours and extra hours shown separately
- [ ] hours actually worked
- [ ] each deduction, itemized
- [ ] units produced each day, if paid by the piece
- [ ] the start and end dates of the pay period
- [ ] your name, address and federal tax ID number (FEIN)

## Daily records you must keep

For each worker ([20 CFR 655.122(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)):

- [ ] field tally records and payroll summaries
- [ ] the type and amount of work done
- [ ] hours offered each day, guaranteed and extra kept separate
- [ ] hours actually worked each day
- [ ] start and stop time each day
- [ ] the reason, if hours worked were fewer than hours offered
- [ ] rate of pay and earnings per pay period
- [ ] the worker's permanent home address (home country address for H-2A workers), plus email and phone when available
- [ ] the amount and reason for each deduction

## How often to pay

At least **twice a month**, or more often if that is the local practice. Your job order states the frequency, and you must stick to it ([655.122(m)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)).

## Deductions

Only deductions required by law or disclosed in your job order are allowed, and all must be reasonable ([655.122(p)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)). Meal charges cannot exceed DOL's daily maximum. Workers can never be charged recruitment fees.

## How long to keep it

- At least **3 years** from the certification date ([20 CFR 655.167](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.167)).
- If records are kept at a central office, you must produce them within **72 hours** of a request.

Most payroll systems can email reports automatically after each pay run. Sending them to one organized place means you are ready the day a request arrives. See [preparing for an H-2A audit](/resources/h-2a-audit-prep).
