H-2A has its own vocabulary. Here are the terms you will see most, in plain English.

**Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR).** The minimum hourly wage DOL sets for H-2A jobs by state and job type. You pay the highest of the AEWR and other required rates. See [AEWR explained](/resources/aewr-explained).

**Agent.** A business that prepares and files H-2A paperwork for a farm. The farm stays the employer. Battenkill Ag is an agent.

**Certification.** DOL's approval of your application. It starts the 3-year clock for keeping records.

**Corresponding worker.** A U.S. worker doing the same work as your H-2A workers. They are owed at least the same pay, benefits and conditions.

**Daily subsistence.** The amount you pay per day for a worker's meals while traveling to and from your farm. See [travel reimbursement](/resources/travel-reimbursement).

**Date of need.** The first day you need H-2A workers. Every deadline counts back from it. See [the H-2A timeline](/resources/h-2a-timeline).

**Debarment.** Being barred from the H-2A program for a period of time, usually after serious violations or failing to respond to an audit.

**ETA-790 and 790A (job order).** The forms that describe the job: duties, pay, hours, housing, meals and other terms. They become the work contract if there is no separate one.

**ETA-9142A.** The Application for Temporary Employment Certification filed with DOL.

**Fifty percent rule.** You must hire qualified U.S. applicants until half the contract period has passed. See [hiring U.S. workers](/resources/hiring-us-workers).

**FLAG.** DOL's online system for filing job orders and applications, and for receiving DOL notices.

**Form I-129.** The petition filed with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) to bring in H-2A workers.

**H-2A labor contractor (H-2ALC).** A business that employs H-2A workers and supplies them to farms. Unlike an agent, it is the employer, and it must post a surety bond.

**Notice of acceptance (NOA).** DOL's notice that your application is complete enough to move forward.

**Notice of deficiency (NOD).** DOL's notice that something in your application needs fixing. You have 5 business days to respond.

**Positive recruitment.** Required efforts to find U.S. workers, such as contacting former workers and any extra steps DOL requires.

**State workforce agency (SWA).** Your state's labor agency. It posts your job order and inspects your housing.

**Three-fourths guarantee.** Your promise to offer at least 75% of the contract hours. See [the three-fourths guarantee](/resources/three-fourths-guarantee).

**Wage and Hour Division (WHD).** The part of DOL that investigates pay, hours, housing and treatment of workers during the season.
