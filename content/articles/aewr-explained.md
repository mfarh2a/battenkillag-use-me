The **Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR)** is the minimum hourly wage DOL sets for H-2A jobs in each state. It exists so that hiring H-2A workers does not push down pay for U.S. farmworkers. For most farms, it is the single biggest number in the H-2A budget.

## Which wage you actually pay

You must pay the **highest** of these rates ([20 CFR 655.120](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.120), [655.122(l)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)):

- the AEWR for your state and job
- the prevailing wage, if DOL has published one
- any agreed union rate
- the federal or state minimum wage

If you pay piece rates, workers must still earn at least the required hourly rate for every hour worked in each pay period.

## How the AEWR changed

In October 2025, DOL changed how the AEWR is calculated ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/02/2025-19365/adverse-effect-wage-rate-methodology-for-the-temporary-employment-of-h-2a-nonimmigrants-in-non-range)). The new method uses a different federal wage survey and splits jobs into two skill levels:

- **Skill Level I** for entry-level farm work
- **Skill Level II** for more experienced roles, at a higher rate

Rates vary widely by state. DOL publishes every current rate on its [AEWR page](https://flag.dol.gov/wage-data/adverse-effect-wage-rates), so check your state's rate before you file.

## The August 2026 court ruling

On August 27, 2026, a federal court in the Eastern District of California ruled that the October 2025 method was unlawful. Current rates **stay in effect** while DOL writes a replacement method. The court left open whether employers could later owe back wages for part of that period. Watch DOL's [AEWR page](https://flag.dol.gov/wage-data/adverse-effect-wage-rates) for updates.

## What this means for your farm

- **Budget with room to spare.** Rates may change before or during your season.
- **Raise pay if the rate goes up mid-contract.** You must match a higher AEWR for the rest of the contract. If it goes down, you cannot cut pay below your job order.
- **Keep clean payroll records.** If back pay is ever ordered, accurate records make it simple to calculate.
