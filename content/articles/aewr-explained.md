The **Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR)** is the minimum hourly wage DOL sets for H-2A jobs. Rates are set by state, job and skill level. For most farms, wages are the biggest number in the H-2A budget.

**Current as of November 2, 2026.** The rates below may be replaced as soon as December 30, 2026. See the court ruling section below.

## Which wage you actually pay

You must pay the **highest** of these rates ([20 CFR 655.120](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.120), [655.122(l)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)):

- the AEWR for your state, job and skill level
- the prevailing wage, if DOL has published one
- any agreed union rate
- the federal, state or local minimum wage

Check your state minimum wage, especially for Skill Level I. In some states it is higher than the AEWR, and it may change on January 1.

If you pay piece rates, workers must still earn at least the required hourly rate for every hour worked in each pay period.

## Skill Level I and Skill Level II

Each job has two rates:

- **Skill Level I** is for entry-level work.
- **Skill Level II** is for more experienced roles and pays more.

Your level depends on the duties performed on most workdays and the qualifications in your job offer. Requirements such as prior experience, a license or a certification can point to Skill Level II, so settle this before you write the job order.

The rates in the table below cover the combined field and livestock category: graders and sorters, agricultural equipment operators, crop and greenhouse farmworkers, farm and ranch animal farmworkers, and hand packers. DOL publishes separate rates for other jobs.

## The housing adjustment

DOL calls this the **H-2A Adverse Compensation Adjustment**. It is an hourly amount **subtracted** from the AEWR for H-2A workers who are provided housing at no cost. It is based on the statewide average fair market rent for a four-bedroom home, and the amount varies by state.

- **It is already built into the H-2A rates below.** Do not subtract it again.
- **It only applies to H-2A workers.** U.S. workers in corresponding employment get the higher U.S. worker rate.
- **It is not a paycheck deduction.** You still provide H-2A housing at no cost to the worker.
- **A minimum wage can still set the floor.** If your state minimum is higher than the adjusted rate, you pay the state minimum.

For example, New York's Skill Level I rate is $16.17 for U.S. workers. Subtract the $2.55 adjustment and the H-2A rate is $13.62. New York's minimum wage is higher than that, so you would pay the minimum wage instead.

## The court ruling and December 30

In August 2026, a federal court in the Eastern District of California ruled that the method behind these rates was unlawful, including how it set the skill levels and the housing adjustment. On October 2, it ordered DOL to issue a new method by **December 30, 2026**. The current method ends that day.

- **Keep paying at least the current rate for now.** Rates stay in effect until new ones are published.
- **Back pay is possible.** DOL says employers owe no back wages at this time. But for September 2, 2026 until the new method is issued, you may owe the difference if workers were paid below the new rates.
- **Keep clean records.** Save hours, pay and contact details for every H-2A and corresponding U.S. worker, including those who have left.

## What this means for your farm

- **Budget with room to spare.** Do not treat today's rate as your final labor cost.
- **Raise pay if the rate goes up mid-contract.** If it goes down, you cannot cut pay below your job order.
- **Check the rate before you file.** Use DOL's [AEWR page](https://flag.dol.gov/wage-data/adverse-effect-wage-rates) for the latest numbers and other job categories.
