Workers leave for many reasons. How you document it decides what you still owe.

## The 2-working-day rule

If a worker **abandons the job** or is **fired for cause**, notify DOL and, for H-2A workers, DHS within **2 working days** ([20 CFR 655.122(n)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)). If you do, you no longer owe that worker:

- return travel and daily subsistence
- the three-fourths hours guarantee

Miss the deadline and you may still owe both.

## When it counts as abandonment

A worker has abandoned the job after **5 consecutive working days** of not reporting for work without your consent.

## Firing for cause

You must be able to show the firing was for a legitimate reason related to the job. Document the rule that was broken, any warnings, and what happened. Keep these records for 3 years.

The 2024 Farmworker Protection Rule added detailed progressive discipline steps. DOL's Wage and Hour Division suspended enforcement of that rule in June 2025 ([DOL bulletin](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/fab/fab2025-2.pdf)), but clear warnings and written records are still the best protection.

## Workers transferring to another employer

Under a DHS rule effective January 17, 2025, an H-2A worker can start work with a new H-2A employer once that employer properly files a new petition. Workers also get a grace period of up to 60 days after their job ends to find new work or leave ([the DHS rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-12-18/pdf/2024-29353.pdf)).

**If a worker leaves you for another employer:** record the last day worked, the reason, and the new employer if you know it.

**If you hire a worker from another farm:** keep a copy of your new petition's receipt, their previous contract details, and their start date.

## Contract impossibility

If fire, weather or another act of God ends the work early and DOL agrees, you pay the three-fourths guarantee up to that date. You also try to move workers to comparable work, and pay return or onward travel if you cannot ([655.122(o)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-655/subpart-B/section-655.122)).
